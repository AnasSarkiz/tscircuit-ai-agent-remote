# Higher-current detector startup qualification gap

The prior Type-C fixture remains an unqualified partial review. TI SN74LVC1G08 requires VCC >=1.65 V for defined operation; Ioff is specified at VCC=0 V. BQ24074 requires EN2 <=0.4 V for guaranteed low and >=1.4 V for guaranteed high. Its input-current selection is live during startup after OUT short checking. A supervisor on one AND input does not establish a guaranteed AND output while the AND gate supply is between 0 and 1.65 V. No actual overcurrent transient has been measured, but the current design cannot yet guarantee USB100 during every ramp. Do not integrate the high-current detector or mark this electrical qualification passed.

Possible simpler board policy within the user's requirements: keep charger EN1 and EN2 physically grounded for USB100, without firmware current override or high-current detector integration. All downstream circuitry would be powered through charger OUT so its input limit includes the entire device load. Existing passive 5.1k Rd and USB-device data connectivity would remain. The protected battery would supplement active loads; operation with a missing/depleted battery would be held off until its readiness/current budget is qualified. Charging would be slow: roughly 6–12 hours for 500–1000mAh, depending on available charging current and taper, not a validated runtime claim. No fast charge requirement is stated in REQUIREMENTS.md or the original brief.

This is a proposed direction, not an approved charger/pack/current specification. Requirements mandate setting final currents only after pack ratings and complete thermal/load budget are established. The sourcing question is still unanswered. Battery TS/protection/current/mechanics remain blocked. No dependency, runtime, footprint or checker modification has been made.

Primary references: TI SN74LVC1G08 Rev AA electrical tables (VCC operating 1.65–5.5 V; Ioff at zero); TI BQ24074 SLUS810N, pages 9, 14, 19 (EN settings, logic limits and power-on selection). PDFs already preserved under evidence/audio-charge-review-2026-10-03/.
